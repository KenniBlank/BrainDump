## Some Terms Involved
---
- `LLM`: Large Language Model
- `RAG`: Retrieval Augmented Generation
- `Chunk`: A smaller, continuous segment of a large text document.
- `Embedding`: The mathematical conversion of human text into high-dimensional numerical vectors.
- `Vector Store`: A specialized database designed to efficiently map and search embedding vectors based on semantic similarity.

---
## The Problem
---
LLM's are pretrained models and as such, they lack access to newer information than the ones that were present during their training.

When asked a question on newer events, the model is unable to answer and in the worst case will hallucinate an answer to satisfy the user.

Example Scenario:

A model is trained at Sep 10 and a new incident takes place on Sep 11. When the model is asked in regards to the event, the model lacks the new information and as such fails to deliver.


---
## Solution:  RAG
---
Since LLM's are frozen in time and are lacking new information, the solution seems simple enough: provide the LLM with the new information and then ask the prompt to get your upto date answer.

> In a way, the models like gemini, chatgpt do this. They retrieve from the web and answer you.

Pipeline: Chunk -> Embed -> Store -> Retrieve -> Augment -> Generate

## Pipeline Steps
---
1. Fetch Document (Self Explanatory)

2. Document Chunk

	Processing documents in their entirety is inefficient — most models have a limited context window, and stuffing the whole document wastes tokens on irrelevant parts + drowns out the actually-relevant bit the answer depends on. To manage this, lengthy texts are divided into smaller segments (say 150 words/tokens per chunk). A deliberate overlap of words (say 30) is maintained between consecutive chunks to ensure surrounding context and critical concepts are not abruptly severed at the boundaries.

3. Embed + Store

	Next, the text chunk is passed through an embedding model. The model translates the underlying semantic meaning of the text into higher-dimensional numerical vectors. These vectors are then plotted and stored within a vector database, such as ChromaDB. This creates a mathematical map where concepts sharing similar themes are clustered closely together, enabling searches based on actual intent rather than fragile keyword matching.

4. Retrieve

	When a user submits a question, that specific query undergoes the exact same embedding process to become a numerical vector. The system queries the vector database to locate the data points situated closest to the query's mathematical position (commonly using cosine similarity). Then, based on the top-k nearest neighboring vectors, the system filters out irrelevant data and isolates the specific document chunks most likely to contain the answer.

5. Augment: Build the prompt

	The final phase involves gathering the retrieved text chunks and seamlessly injecting them, alongside the user's original question, into a prompt template. This prompt acts as reference sheet for the LLM. By forcing the generative model to base its final response on the provided context block, the RAG framework suppresses hallucinations and generates a factually grounded output.

6. Generate: Use the prompt to generate the final answer

## What's Next?
---

This is the simplest version of RAG.

From here:
- **Reranking** — re-score retrieved chunks for better precision
- **Hybrid search** — combine keyword + semantic retrieval
- **Evaluation (RAGAS)** — measure whether retrieval is actually good
- **Agentic RAG** — let the model decide when and what to retrieve
